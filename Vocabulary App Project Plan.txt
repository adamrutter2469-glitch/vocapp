A personal vocabulary learning app with spaced repetition + AI-graded definitions + persistent word history.

What I envision

You'd open something like:

Vocabulary Trainer

[ Quiz Me ] [ Add Word ] [ My Words ] [ Progress ]

Then the quiz experience might be:

Word:

Fastidious

Type your definition:

[ __________________________________________ ]

Submit

The app evaluates your answer and gives you something like:

Your answer:
"Someone who is extremely careful about details."

Accuracy: 92%

Dictionary definition:
Very attentive to detail and accuracy.

Why: Your definition captures the core meaning very well. You didn't explicitly mention cleanliness/orderliness, which is another common aspect of the word.

Then:

Example:

"She was fastidious about keeping her financial records organized."

And potentially an image associated with the concept.

I'd break the app into 5 pieces
1. Word database

This is the foundation.

Something like:

Field	Example
word	fastidious
definition	Very attentive to detail...
part_of_speech	adjective
synonyms	meticulous, exacting
example	She was fastidious...
image_url	...
date_added	2026-08-14
times_quizzed	12
avg_accuracy	87%
last_quizzed	2026-08-13
retention_score	0.82

And then a separate table for every quiz attempt:

word	date	your_answer	accuracy
fastidious	Aug 10	Very careful...	91%
fastidious	Aug 12	Someone who is picky...	73%
fastidious	Aug 14	Extremely careful...	96%

That second table is important because it lets you eventually answer things like:

"How many times did I get fastidious right?"

and

"Which words am I struggling with?"

2. The dictionary/content engine

When you add a word:

Add Word

fastidious

The app could automatically retrieve:

Dictionary definition
Part of speech
Pronunciation
Synonyms
Example sentences
Etymology, if you want it
Image

You shouldn't have to manually enter all of that.

You could simply type:

fastidious

and hit Add.

The app does the rest.

3. The AI grading system

This is probably the coolest part.

You don't want to simply compare your response to a dictionary definition.

For example:

Target

Fastidious = very attentive to detail and accuracy.

You answer:

"Someone who is extremely picky and careful about how things are done."

A simple string comparison would say they're completely different.

An LLM can recognize that your answer demonstrates understanding.

I'd actually have the AI return several metrics:

accuracy: 91
core_meaning: 95
completeness: 85

But your interface could simply display:

91% Correct

And perhaps:

What you got right

Extremely careful
Concerned with details

What you missed

Accuracy/precision is an important component

This also gives you a much better measure of whether you've actually learned the word.

4. Spaced repetition

This is where I'd make your app significantly better than simply having a "quiz me" button.

The app should learn which words you know.

For example:

Word	Accuracy	Last quiz	Next quiz
fastidious	96%	Today	3 days
quixotic	88%	Yesterday	2 days
obdurate	61%	Today	Tomorrow
perspicacious	42%	Today	Later today

So when you click:

Quiz Me

the app doesn't randomly select a word.

It calculates:

Which word am I most likely to forget right now?

and gives you that one.

You'd essentially be building your own Anki-style vocabulary system, except with AI grading rather than multiple-choice/self-grading.

Why Streamlit?

Because you could build the entire interface in Python.

You don't need to learn:

React
JavaScript
TypeScript
Node
CSS frameworks

You could literally have something like:

st.title("Vocabulary Trainer")


word = get_next_word()


st.header(word)


answer = st.text_area("Define this word:")


if st.button("Submit"):
    result = grade_definition(word, answer)
    st.write(result)

And Streamlit turns that into a web interface.

That's very achievable.

One thing I'd do differently from a typical vocabulary app

I'd store your actual definition attempts permanently.

That's important.

Imagine six months from now you look at:

Quixotic

Your history might say:

Aug 14: 58%
"Someone who has unrealistic ideas."

Aug 21: 76%
"Someone who pursues an idealistic goal despite it probably being unrealistic."

Sep 4: 94%
"Extremely idealistic and unrealistic, especially in pursuit of an impossible goal."

That's a really satisfying way to see yourself actually learning the vocabulary.

And you could eventually have:

Retention

Quixotic

Initial accuracy: 58%

Current accuracy: 94%

Attempts: 7

Retention: Strong

And importantly: you could probably keep the infrastructure essentially free

For a personal app, I wouldn't start by paying for a server.

You could develop it locally first:

your computer
    │
    ├── Python
    ├── Streamlit
    └── DuckDB

Then once it's working, put the database/storage in the cloud and deploy the application.

The potentially non-free component would be AI grading, because every submitted definition requires an API call. But we can design that to be extremely cheap, and potentially experiment with free/local models depending on how sophisticated you want the grading to be.

One other idea I really like

Instead of asking:

Define "fastidious."

occasionally reverse it:

Which word means "extremely attentive to detail and accuracy"?

Then you type:

fastidious

and it grades that too.

And eventually:

Fill in the blank:
"He was so ______ about his work that he spent 30 minutes checking a single spreadsheet."

This gives you recall + recognition + contextual usage, which is much better for retention than one quiz format.
